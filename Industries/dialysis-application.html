DIALYSIS WATER MANAGEMENT

Supporting Water Quality inDialysis Facilities

Water quality is an essential part of haemodialysis operations. Dialysis facilities require carefully designed water treatment systems to ensure that water used for preparing dialysis fluids meets the applicable chemical and microbiological quality requirements.

The water treatment arrangement may include pretreatment equipment, reverse osmosis, storage and distribution systems, along with regular monitoring and maintenance.

Ozone can be considered as a controlled disinfection option for compatible water storage and distribution systems. Its use requires appropriate system design, monitoring and safeguards before the water system is returned to service.

OZONE DISINFECTION

Key Characteristics ofOzone-Based Disinfection

Ozone has specific disinfection properties that can be useful in appropriately engineered water system applications.

Microbial Control

Ozone can inactivate a range of microorganisms under suitable concentration and contact conditions.

Strong Oxidizing Action

Ozone is a powerful oxidizing agent with established applications in water disinfection and treatment.

Decomposes After Treatment

Ozone naturally decomposes over time. However, residual levels must still be verified before the system is returned to dialysis service.

Controlled Application

A suitable ozone system can be designed around the specific water system, equipment and treatment requirements.

Monitoring Integration

System-Level Design

SAFETY AND QUALITY

Patient Safety andWater Quality Come First

Dialysis water systems require strict quality management. Ozone disinfection must be engineered and controlled so that patients are not exposed to unsafe disinfectant residues.

Any proposed ozone application must be reviewed for compatibility with the water treatment equipment and applicable dialysis water requirements.

  • Appropriate system compatibility assessment.
  • Controlled disinfection operation and clear status indication.
  • Safeguards to prevent patient exposure during disinfection.
  • Verification of disinfectant residuals before returning the system to service.
  • Regular chemical and microbiological water quality monitoring.

Important: Ozone disinfection is not a substitute for reverse osmosis, required water purification, microbiological testing or dialysis facility quality management. Ozone must not be allowed to reach patients through the dialysis water or dialysis fluid. Use only a validated, compatible system under qualified facility supervision.
